# The batch of 2026-11-19

The work settled with the user on 2026-11-19, to be built together. Each item
has its own design document, which is authoritative for it. This page says what
each is, why, what it decided, what is still open, and the order to build them
in.

## 1. The items

### 1.1 A request's steps, and the request turn that anchors them

**Document:** `docs/request_steps.md`.

**Why.** A long agentic request loses its own notes. A `journal_note` is
visible only while the turn that wrote it is projected, and the fill sheds a
request's middle tool turns first. Entries come too late and too coarse to
carry a request in progress. Measured on the yenbot worker loop of 2026-11-18:
the same finding was noted six times, and the plan was re-derived every round.

**Decided:**

- **Two headed parts in the journal.** *Journal history* holds the entries.
  *Current request* holds the notes this conversation wrote within the request
  in progress, as numbered steps.
- **Steps:** this conversation's only, the newest 64, each clipped to 400
  characters, and gone when the request finishes. Its entry replaces them.
- **The forced note stays.** Journaling depends on it.
- **"Still open"** shows only when something is open. The line that says the
  journal ends there stays.
- **The request turn is never windowed out.** That already holds today: the
  selection keeps the newest exchange, and the fill keeps its first turn as a
  floor even past the room. An end-to-end test is to pin it. The dormant
  summary-tree path does not hold it.

**Open:** whether the steps go when the request finishes, or when its closing
entry seals (§4 there).

### 1.2 A job across its agent's restart

**Document:** `docs/jobs.md` §6.1.

**Why.** `job_status` called the proven deploy of 8088a2457 lost. The trial's
restart of the agent landed between the status's two calls to it, and every
agent error, a transient 503 included, becomes the terminal `lost`. The real
losses of 2026-11-17 and 11-18 predate 7361debde, which fixed them.

**Decided:**

- An unreachable agent is restarting, not finished: `job_status` keeps
  waiting.
- Every agent call on a deploy's path falls back to keep's board.
- A run parked on keep is not lost before the new process takes its queue back.

### 1.3 A failed install, and what the status says about it

**Document:** `docs/cluster_status_story.md`.

**Why.** A trial that missed its 10-minute window on WhiteWorld left the member
connected below the floor, routed nothing, and installing nothing, for good.
The failed build is refused for ever (`refused.json`), and the gateway is never
told. Meanwhile WhiteWorld's `5207` connector went on serving beside
BlackWorld's `5211`. The status said "loading or stuck", "upgrading" and "left
behind" at once.

**Decided:**

- **Record and retry.** Each failure is recorded with its kind: slow, died or
  cancelled. It is retried three times, at 10, 30 and 90 minutes.
- **Serve the old build** between attempts, while older builds may serve.
- **Then out, with the reason:** stranded, closed 4410.
- **Nothing serves from a member that is not serving.** The connector follows
  its daemon out of rotation, and the daemon's writing loops wait on one
  `serving` signal.
- **The status is an account per machine,** in order, with times, then the
  present. `MemberState::Behind` replaces the misleading `upgrading`, and the
  rollout line counts installing, serving-older and behind members.

**Open:**

- WhiteWorld's exact path, which has to be read on WhiteWorld.
- BlackWorld's 964 unreceipted events, from 14:36 UTC on 2026-11-18 (§6 there).

### 1.4 Reprojection after the pool-first revision

**Document:** `docs/archived/reprojection_bottlenecks.md`.

**Why.** Measured on BlackWorld with one chat:

- 48 of 49 reprojections rebuilt, at 338 ms each: 27% of the minute.
- The belief scan took 175 ms of each rebuild, and the pool refresh 93 ms,
  re-reading 2.8–127 MB of index pages a second.
- One stream decoded at ~17 tokens/s against a ~31 tokens/s forward bound.

**Decided.** Each part is optimized where it runs, on the scheduler thread,
with nothing moved off it, deferred or slowed:

- **Step 0:** attribute every part.
- **Step 1:** the pool keeps a piece's pages while it is pooled.
- **Step 2:** one scan launch over every group, with each piece's index built
  when it enters the pool.
- **Step 3:** a rebuild that costs what changed.

The pool-first selection is not reverted.

**Done when:**

- reprojection is under 5% of the minute;
- a rebuild takes under 60 ms;
- decode is within 10% of its forward bound;
- the battery stays at or above 51.0%.

## 2. Also measured today

**The dependency battery on the pool-first projection:** 51.0% mean recall and
8 full marks, against 46.2% and 6 on 2026-11-18. That is inside the noise of 24
questions (`docs/results/dependency_battery_2026-11-19.md`). The pool ranks a
piece by strength summed over its pages, so a large file outranks a smaller one
that answers better. `resolver.rs`, 264 pages, ranked second among readings.
Whether to rank per page, or to cap a piece's share, is for the user to decide.
It is not part of this batch.
