## Life Story

Bramble grew up in a small hill village on the northern edge of the kingdom, the third child of a stonecutter and a weaver. His father, Ashton, left when Bramble was six and never returned, leaving his mother Wren to raise him and his older sister Sorrel alone. As a child, he showed an early fascination with nature — watching flowers, insects, and the landscape with unrelenting attention. By the time he reached his teens, he had developed a deep knowledge of plants, particularly through his mentorship under Heddle, the inn's gardener. He began formal apprenticeship at fourteen and continued working in the garden after school, eventually taking full charge at sixteen. At nineteen, he is still working at the inn garden, managing it as both a professional and a personal inheritance. His knowledge of horticulture has grown steadily, shaped by observation, practice, and loss — especially the death of Heddle, who taught him everything he knows. He is quiet, methodical, and deeply connected to the land, carrying Heddle’s trowel as a symbol of both memory and responsibility. His work has earned recognition, but his greatest legacy remains the sweet pea cross he has been cultivating for years.

Bramble grew up on the edge of a hill village in the northern kingdom. His father was a stonecutter, his mother a weaver; he was their third child. ## Life Story

Bramble grew up on the edge of a hill village in the northern part of the kingdom. The third child of a stonecutter and a weaver, he was raised by his mother after his father left when Bramble was six. His early life was marked by quiet observation — watching the world from the cradle, the kitchen table, and the garden, absorbing details with a sharp mind and unspoken curiosity.
